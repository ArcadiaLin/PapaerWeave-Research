\begin{abstract}
LLM agents increasingly perform literature-based research tasks. Accumulating
their outputs in a long-lived shared store would let later agents build on
prior work. Existing agent memory systems, however, are largely designed for a
single owner and resolve conflicting writes by recency or internal judgment.
When writers do not coordinate, this assumption fails: absent appropriate
mechanisms, new content often remains unlinked to existing content, and store
quality degrades as writes accumulate. We argue that a shared store should not
adjudicate correctness but should keep every write accountable: attributable to
its writer and traceable to its evidence and prior content. We present
PaperWeave, a data management system for paper-derived knowledge. It separates
what a paper states from what a writer judges: source-grounded records hold one
value, revisable by any writer with a source anchor and a reason, while
interpretations are attributed to their writers and coexist without
adjudication. PaperWeave adapts entity resolution, view invalidation, and data
versioning to agent writers to maintain accountability as uncoordinated writes
accumulate. We design an evaluation on an intent-grounded workload to measure
store integrity and downstream answer accuracy as the number of writers grows.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

LLM agents are increasingly being developed to assist researchers across the
scientific workflow, from finding relevant studies and understanding their
methods to comparing experimental results, synthesizing findings, verifying
claims, and obtaining implementation resources~\citeneeded{research agents}.
These tasks repeatedly extract and interpret overlapping information from
papers, including methods, experimental results, and supporting evidence.
Popular methods, datasets, and benchmarks recur across research projects, so
agents often redo costly reading and comparison work even when earlier sessions
have already extracted relevant results and evidence. These outputs can inform
later studies, making scientific research a natural setting for accumulating
knowledge across tasks and over time.

Long-term memory lets agents retain useful information from prior interactions
\cite{2025-Zep}, and recent work has begun to share reusable experience across
agents~\cite{2024-MemorySharing,2025-CollaborativeMemory,2026-SkillClaw}.
This line of work establishes the value of cross-agent reuse. Research tasks
produce evidence-bearing knowledge about papers, methods, and experiments.
Its value depends on links to stable identities and source evidence that
remain usable as writers add, revise, and reinterpret content. A shared
research memory therefore calls for database-style management of identity,
provenance, change, and retrieval.

This need is especially tractable in scholarly research. Paper-derived
entities have external identity anchors such as DOIs and arXiv IDs; revisions,
errata, and retractions expose knowledge change; and public sources and later
research choices make many records checkable. These properties create both
substantial opportunity for reuse and a practical basis for evaluating whether
an accumulated store remains findable and reliable. Scholarly knowledge
graphs~\citeneeded{ORKG}, in turn, rely on human curators and templates, while
data systems for scholarly corpora~\cite{2026-AgenticScholar} operate over
documents rather than knowledge contributed by many independent agents.

In this setting, writers may be agents of different people or projects, or
independent sessions of one researcher over time. Each writes for its own task
without knowing what others have added or how they interpreted the literature;
the shared store is their only channel, and later readers were not present when
its content was written. We call these writers \emph{uncoordinated} in this
semantic sense: writes may be serialized, while atomicity and isolation remain
the responsibility of the underlying database. The challenge is to preserve a
useful shared body of knowledge as these independent contributions accumulate.

Many agent memory systems are organized around the state an individual agent
needs across interactions, increasingly using knowledge graphs with entity
resolution and temporal validity. For a single owner, resolving conflicting
writes by recency or internal judgment can be reasonable: a newer write may
reflect newer knowledge. Under independent writing, however, recency alone
cannot establish which account is correct, and different interpretations may
both be defensible under different conditions.

Without mechanisms for shared accumulation, new content tends to remain
unlinked to, and uninformed by, existing content (Figure~\ref{fig:scenario}).
The sources themselves also change through new arXiv versions, errata, and
retractions. The same method is recorded
twice under different names, or two datasets that share a name are merged into
one; in our pilot corpus on long-term time-series forecasting, for example, the
\emph{Weather} dataset used by Informer~\citeneeded{Informer} differs from the
dataset of the same name commonly used by later papers. A later reading of a
result silently replaces an earlier, conflicting one, and a comparison built on
numbers that a paper has since revised continues to be served with no sign that
its input changed. We hypothesize that, as writers and writes accumulate, such
a store degrades both in store integrity, through duplicate and wrongly merged
entities, dangling references, unnoticed conflicts, and stale derived results,
and in the accuracy of the answers later readers derive from it. These answers
can be wrong while appearing well supported.

The store cannot avoid this by deciding which writer is right. It has no better
access to the truth than its writers, and ranking content by usage or citation
would favor what was written first or most often. We therefore argue for
accountability rather than authority: a shared store should not adjudicate
correctness, but should keep every write attributable to its writer and
traceable to its evidence and to the prior content it builds on. Authority
remains outside the store, in fixed versions of the source papers and in
external identifiers such as DOIs and arXiv IDs. This principle separates what
a paper states from what a writer judges. \emph{Source-grounded records} state
what a paper says, such as an experiment, a claim, or a contribution as its
authors present it. Their correctness means fidelity to the source and can be
checked against anchors in the paper, so a disagreement among writers is a
temporary error, resolved by a revision that cites an anchor and a reason while
the prior value remains in history. \emph{Interpretations} state what a writer
judges, such as an observation, a judgment that one claim supports or opposes
another, or a derived artifact such as a cross-paper comparison. They have no
single correct value, so competing interpretations coexist, each attributed to
the writer that formed it.

Realizing this principle raises five challenges.
\textbf{C1, identity under independent writes:} identities must converge across
writers without wrong merges, under rules that are explicit, checkable, and
uniform.
\textbf{C2, self-describing contributions:} because writers share no context,
every contribution must carry its conditions, evidence, and inputs, so that
readers who were not present can use or check it.
\textbf{C3, disagreement and change:} conflicts and staleness must be surfaced,
neither overwritten nor rejected.
\textbf{C4, isolated work, shared delivery:} project work needs a stable base
and must deliver its results back without breaking the shared store.
\textbf{C5, discoverability across writers:} content stored for one purpose
must be findable, within a bounded context, by a reader with another purpose
who knows neither the writer's wording nor its organization.
C1--C4 determine whether readers can trust and build on what others wrote; C5
determines whether they can find it.

We present \textbf{PaperWeave}, a data management system for paper-derived
knowledge that addresses these challenges. General-purpose agents use it
through the Model Context Protocol (MCP)~\citeneeded{MCP}, under a strict
division of labor: agents read papers and make every semantic judgment, while
PaperWeave validates structure and references, executes operations, persists
artifacts, and records lineage and state changes, without any internal LLM
reasoning. Its data model and write operators enforce the two layers:
source-grounded records hold one value, revisable by any writer with a source
anchor and a reason, while interpretations belong to their writers, who alone
may modify them. PaperWeave keeps a thin graph of identity anchors, references,
and commits, and places rich content in artifact documents whose structure
agents declare by contract.

None of PaperWeave's mechanisms is new in isolation, and we claim no new theory
of provenance, versioning, or concurrency. Our contribution is to select
classical mechanisms and adapt them to agent writers, whose errors are semantic
and fluent and whose derived content is costly and nondeterministic to
recompute. \emph{Entity resolution}~\citeneeded{entity resolution} addresses C1,
but adjudication returns to the writer: at write time, PaperWeave returns
candidate matches to the writer, which either reuses an existing object or
declares the new one distinct, and the decision is recorded in the commit.
\emph{Provenance}~\citeneeded{W3C PROV; nanopublications} addresses C2: every
artifact declares its inputs, and source materials are pinned by content hash.
\emph{View invalidation}~\citeneeded{materialized view maintenance} addresses C3
for derived content: PaperWeave flags artifacts whose inputs have since been
revised instead of recomputing them, and because readers rarely query
provenance on their own, conflicts and staleness travel with query results.
\emph{Data versioning}~\cite{2026-Git4Data}\citeneeded{OrpheusDB; Decibel}
addresses C3 and C4: every write is a commit with before and after values, any
past state can be reconstructed, and projects fork from a fixed state and push
their results back.
% C4 的 fork / push 目前只有设计（docs/designs/v2/project_views.md），尚未实现。
For C5, PaperWeave derives its retrieval surface and access operators from
research intents, combining structural navigation with semantic
search~\citeneeded{dataspaces; keyword search over databases}.
In this sense, writers collaborate without coordinating: the invariants that
PaperWeave maintains, namely that every write is attributed, every reference
resolves, and every derived artifact declares its inputs, can be checked at
each write against the current store, without knowledge of what other writers
intend; correctness, which would require such knowledge, is left outside the
store.
% 上面一句试图回应标题引出的 I-confluence / CALM 期待，措辞待定。

We scope our study to scientific literature, which makes the problem both
pronounced and measurable. Writes overlap heavily on popular methods, datasets,
and benchmarks; identities are hard to resolve yet anchored by external
identifiers; disagreement and revision arise naturally; and public gold
resources, together with the choices that later papers actually made, provide
ground truth. Our claims and results cover this domain only. The mechanisms are
domain-agnostic by design, and we discuss generalization separately.

We evaluate PaperWeave on an intent-grounded workload. Six research intents,
each backed by public task sources, generate both writer tasks, so that writers
with different purposes write to the same objects at different granularities,
and reader probes, so that probes are grounded in real tasks rather than
tailored to our system. Writers are runs of an unmodified general-purpose agent
that take turns on a shared store, interleaved with events of revision,
retraction, and divergence. We compare PaperWeave with files under Git in their
strongest configuration, retrieval-augmented generation, the same schema on a
graph database queried with Cypher, and an agent memory system, and we ablate
its mechanisms one at a time. Our central hypothesis, which the experiments can
falsify, is that PaperWeave's store integrity and downstream answer accuracy
degrade significantly more slowly than those of the baselines as the number of
writers grows. We also test whether construction cost, shifted from use to
build, amortizes across later readers.
% TODO: 实验完成后在此报告主要发现；agent-memory 基线尚未写入 evaluation_draft.md。

In summary, this paper makes the following contributions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Problem and workload.} We formulate shared accumulation of
    paper-derived knowledge by uncoordinated agent writers, and give an
    intent-grounded workload of writer tasks and reader probes derived from six
    research intents, together with events of revision, retraction,
    divergence, and branching.
  \item \textbf{Design.} We present PaperWeave, which separates
    source-grounded records from interpretations and adapts entity resolution,
    provenance, view invalidation, and data versioning to agent writers, with a
    retrieval surface and access operators derived from research intents.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation.} We test whether these mechanisms slow the
    degradation of shared accumulation against strong baselines under equal
    access and budgets, isolate the effect of each mechanism by ablation, and
    measure whether construction cost amortizes across readers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Accumulation Model (Draft)}
\label{sec:model-draft}

\paragraph{States and commits.}
PaperWeave stores a property graph together with a store of source materials.
Writes are serialized commits $c_1, c_2, \ldots$; $G_t$ denotes the state after
$c_t$, and $G_0$ the seed state. A commit
\[
c_t = (\mathit{author}_t,\ \mathit{op}_t,\ \mathit{msg}_t,\ \Delta_t,\
  \mathit{meta}_t)
\]
records who wrote, through which operator, why, and what changed. The
changeset $\Delta_t$ lists the value of every touched node and relationship
before and after the write, with $\bot$ for absence; $\mathit{meta}_t$ holds the
confirmations the write relied on, such as distinctness declarations, revision
anchors, and reasons. Because $\Delta_t$ keeps both values, every earlier state
$G_s$ with $s<t$ can be reconstructed from $G_t$. No write reaches the store
except through a commit.

\paragraph{Layers.}
Entities and Concepts are identity objects. Every other record $o$ has a layer
$\lambda(o)\in\{\mathsf{grd},\mathsf{int}\}$. Experiments and records with
$\texttt{stated\_by}=\texttt{paper}$ (Claims, Contributions, and
\texttt{SUPPORTS} and \texttt{OPPOSES} relationships) are source-grounded.
Observations, records with $\texttt{stated\_by}=\texttt{agent}$, and Artifacts
are interpretive. An interpretive record other than an Artifact carries the
writer that formed it, $\mathit{fb}(o)$. An Artifact $a$ is a sequence of
immutable versions $a@1, a@2, \ldots$; each version is a document registered as
a material, and $\mathrm{head}_G(a)$ is the commit that formed its current
version in $G$.

\textsc{CheckIn} accepts documents of five types: Leaderboard,
CompareSettings, Verify, Summarize, and Generate. Each type declares an
identity key, or none, and a row key. CompareSettings and Verify record each
judgment as $\mathsf T$, $\mathsf F$, or $\mathsf U$. $\mathsf T$ and $\mathsf F$

\subsection{Search: Semantics and Execution}
\label{sec:search-draft}

\paragraph{Semantics.}
For family $T$, allowed scope $S$, and declared structural predicate $\phi$,
define the feasible candidates and the logical ranked result as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:constrained-retrieval}
\begin{aligned}
C_G(T,S,\phi)
  &= \{o\in S : \operatorname{family}(o)=T,\ G\models\phi(o)\},\\
\operatorname{Search}^{*}_G(q,T,S,\phi,k)
  &= \operatorname{TopK}_{\preceq_{T,q}}\bigl(C_G(T,S,\phi)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\preceq_{T,q}$ is the declared family-specific ordering: exact matches
first, in a declared priority, then the fused order of the semantic channels,
with ties broken by identifier. Without a text query, the operation enumerates
feasible objects. A relevance score cannot compensate for a failed hard
constraint, and an absent relation means only that the snapshot contains no
matching record.

\paragraph{Retrieval surface.}
The predicates $\phi$ and channels available for $T$ form its retrieval
surface $R_T$: the part of an object's logical description that the store can
match, filter, or rank on. Each field declares its type, its missing-value
semantics, and the operations it supports; agents may read the whole
description, while the store evaluates only declared operations. Fields may be
backed by relationships, and some must be. Whether a code repository
implements a method is not a property of the repository: two stores can agree
on every property of a repository and still disagree on whether it answers
``implementations of method $m$''. A surface that omits the relationship
cannot answer that query, however its results are ranked.
% TODO: 各 family 的 R_T 以表列出，来源 intents_decompose.md §2.2 与 operators.md §3.1。

\paragraph{Execution.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:search} executes Equation~\ref{eq:constrained-retrieval}.
Every channel recalls only within $C_G(T,S,\phi)$, so the hard constraints hold
by construction. Recall is bounded: each semantic channel contributes at most
$n$ candidates, so the output is drawn from the pool
$\widehat C_B = E\cup\bigcup_h L_h \subseteq C_G(T,S,\phi)$ and can miss
feasible objects that no channel recalled. The vector index is built per
top-level label and filtered after retrieval, so it oversamples and may still
lose feasible candidates. Algorithm~\ref{alg:search} therefore reports the
pool, the oversampling, failed channels, and truncation; it does not claim
exact top-$k$.

\begin{algorithm}[tbp]
\caption{\textsc{Search}: constrained hybrid retrieval}
\label{alg:search}
\raggedright
\textbf{Input:} state $G$; family $T$; query $q$ (identifier, mention, text,
or $\bot$); predicate $\phi$; scope $S$; budget $k$; offset $o$.\\
\textbf{Parameters:} pool size $n$ per channel; RRF constant $r$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Record the head commit $h_0$. Compile $\phi$ and $S$ into a graph
    pattern $\Pi$ for $C_G(T,S,\phi)$.
  \item If $q=\bot$: return the objects matching $\Pi$, ordered by
    identifier, from offset $o$ to $o+k$.
  \item $E\gets$ objects matching $\Pi$ whose registered identifier equals
    $q.\mathit{identifier}$ or whose name key equals that of
    $q.\mathit{mention}$, in declared priority. \hfill\emph{Exact; unscored.}
  \item For each semantic channel $h$ of $T$ (name lexical, full text,
    vector): $L_h\gets$ the top $n$ objects matching $\Pi$ by $h$; the vector
    channel retrieves more than $n$ and filters by $\Pi$. On failure, record
    $h$ as failed and continue.
  \item For each $x\in\bigcup_h L_h\setminus E$:
    $s(x)\gets\sum_{h: x\in L_h} 1/(r+\mathrm{rank}_h(x))$.
  \item $\mathit{ranked}\gets E$ followed by the rest in decreasing $s$, ties
    by identifier.
  \item $P\gets\mathit{ranked}[o:o+k]$. Build each result's view:
    identifying fields and excerpts of retrieval fields within the size
    limit, $\mathrm{Stale}_G$ for each Artifact
    (Eq.~\ref{eq:stale}), revision notes for each grounded record.
  \item Record the head commit $h_1$; the snapshot is $h_0$ if $h_0=h_1$, and
    unknown otherwise.
  \item Return $P$ with match evidence (exact match or per-channel ranks),
    coverage (pool, oversampling, failed channels), the snapshot, and a
    continuation if $\mathit{ranked}$ extends beyond $o+k$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Resolve and Traverse}
\label{sec:resolve-traverse-draft}

\textsc{Resolve} maps a mention to Entity or Concept references in three
stages: a registered identifier, then the exact name key, then semantic
neighbours fused from the same three channels as \textsc{Search}. A unique hit
in the first two stages is \textsf{resolved}; several hits are
\textsf{ambiguous}; semantic neighbours alone are \textsf{candidates}. The
store sets no similarity threshold, so ``not in the store'' is a conclusion the
agent draws by rejecting each candidate. When channels fail and no reference
results, the status is \textsf{unprocessed}, never \textsf{none}. In write mode,
\textsc{Resolve} continues to the semantic stage even after a resolved hit;
\textsc{Commit} uses this mode to find identity candidates
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:commit}).

\textsc{Traverse} follows declared relations from confirmed references. A hop
$h=(\mathit{rel},\mathit{dir},\mathit{kinds},\mathit{where},\mathit{edge},
\mathit{depth})$ matches an edge of a type in $\mathit{rel}$, in direction
$\mathit{dir}$, with edge properties satisfying $\mathit{edge}$, ending at an
object of a type in $\mathit{kinds}$ that satisfies $\mathit{where}$; for
transitive relations, a hop may match a path of up to $\mathit{depth}$ edges
without repeating an edge. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:traverse}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Traverse}_G(X, h_1\cdots h_n)={}
  &\{x_0 e_1 x_1\cdots e_n x_n : x_0\in X,\\
  &\ \ (x_{i-1},e_i,x_i)\models_G h_i \text{ for } 1\le i\le n\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Each hop is checked against the model's endpoint table before execution, and
an impossible hop is a contract error, not an empty result. The result keeps
every path as match evidence, and its object views carry
$\mathrm{Stale}_G$ for every Artifact on a path. The budget $k$ counts paths.

\section{Writes, Versions, and Shared Artifacts (Draft)}
\label{sec:versions-draft}

\subsection{Admissible Writes}
\label{sec:admissible-draft}

Let $c$ be a write submitted by author $u$ against the current state $G$, and
$G'=c(G)$. For a new Entity or Concept $o$, let $\mathrm{Key}_G(o)$ be the
existing objects that share its exact name key or an external identifier,
$\mathrm{Cand}_G(o)$ the semantic neighbours that \textsc{Resolve} returns for
$o$ in write mode, and $\mathrm{Dist}_c(o)$ the existing objects that $c$
declares distinct from $o$.

% TODO: 引言中 ``interpretations belong to their writers, who alone may modify
% them'' 只对 W5 的前半句成立；共同产物允许他人修改，须写理由。
\begin{definition}[Admissible write]
\label{def:admissible}
A write $c$ by $u$ is \emph{admissible} in $G$ if it satisfies W1--W6.
\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{W1}] \emph{Attribution.} $u$ is nonempty, and $\Delta_c$
    enters the store only as part of $c$.
  \item[\textbf{W2}] \emph{Resolvable references and declared inputs.} Every
    reference made by a touched object resolves in $G'$: relationship
    endpoints exist; a source reference names a registered material whose
    content hash matches and whose line range is in bounds; a record
    reference $a\#k$ names a row of the current version of $a$. For each
    Artifact version $a@v$ that $c$ writes,
    $\mathrm{USED}(a@v)=\mathrm{refs}(\mathit{doc}(a@v))$, each input pinned to
    the material version read at write time.
  \item[\textbf{W3}] \emph{Identity.} Every new Entity or Concept $o$ has
    $\mathrm{Key}_G(o)=\varnothing$ and
    $\mathrm{Cand}_G(o)\subseteq\mathrm{Dist}_c(o)$. For every Artifact type
    $p$ and key $\kappa$,
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:artifact-key}
    \left|\{a\in\operatorname{Artifacts}(G'):
      a.\mathit{op}=p\ \land\ a.\mathit{key}=\kappa\}\right|\leq 1.
    \end{equation}
  \item[\textbf{W4}] \emph{Grounded revision.} If $\Delta_c$ modifies or
    deletes a record $o$ with $\lambda(o)=\mathsf{grd}$, then
    $\mathit{meta}_c$ gives a reason, and for a modification also an anchor
    that resolves in the material of $o$'s paper.
  \item[\textbf{W5}] \emph{Accountable interpretation.} If $\Delta_c$ modifies
    the content or outgoing relationships of an interpretive record $o$ that
    is not an Artifact, or deletes it, then $u=\mathit{fb}(o)$. If $c$
    modifies or deletes Artifact rows last edited by a writer other than $u$,
    $\mathit{meta}_c$ gives a reason.
  \item[\textbf{W6}] \emph{Current base.} A write that updates Artifact $a$
    names the base $\mathrm{head}_G(a)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

Each condition is decided from $c$ and from $G$ restricted to the objects $c$
touches, their neighbourhoods, their candidates, and the heads of the Artifacts
it updates. None refers to another writer's pending work or intent. This is the
sense in which writers collaborate without coordinating. Admissibility does
not imply correctness: an admissible write can still misread a paper, and the
store leaves that judgment to its writers and readers.

\subsection{Executing Commit and CheckIn}
\label{sec:commit-draft}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:commit} decides admissibility and applies the write. The two
write operators share it: \textsc{Commit} takes a graph document and stores its
records in the graph; \textsc{CheckIn} takes Artifact documents and stores each
as written, as a new version. Steps marked with one operator apply only to it.
Without \textit{apply}, the call only validates; both modes run the same
checks, so writers see warnings before a version is created.
Table~\ref{tab:blocking} lists the blocking items and the writer's response to
each. The store never resolves a blocking item itself.

\begin{algorithm}[tbp]
\caption{\textsc{Commit} and \textsc{CheckIn}: decide admissibility, then
apply atomically}
\label{alg:commit}
\raggedright
\textbf{Input:} state $G$; a graph document, or Artifact documents $D$;
author $u$; message $m$; confirmations $\kappa$; flag \textit{apply}.\\
\textbf{Output:} blocking items $P$; or changes and warnings; or a commit.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Parse the input into a changeset $\Delta$ against $G$; add format and
    reference errors to $P$. \hfill W2
  \item \textsc{Commit}: for each new Entity or Concept $o$,
    $(\mathit{refs},\mathit{similar})\gets
    \textsc{Resolve}(o.\mathit{identifier}, o.\mathit{name}, o.\mathit{text};
    \mathit{mode}{=}\mathit{write})$. If $\mathit{refs}\ne\varnothing$, add a
    conflict; if $\mathit{similar}\setminus\kappa.\mathrm{distinct}(o)\ne
    \varnothing$, add a pending item carrying these candidates. \hfill W3
  \item \textsc{CheckIn}: for each $d\in D$, if $d$ updates $a$ and
    $d.\mathit{base}\ne\mathrm{head}_G(a)$, add a conflict; if the key of $d$
    belongs to another Artifact, add a conflict carrying it. Resolve the
    references in $d$ into $\mathrm{USED}(d)$, pinning each input to its
    current version. Compare $d$ with its base by row key, and let $O$ be
    the changed or deleted rows last edited by a writer other than $u$.
    \hfill W2, W3, W6
  \item \textsc{Commit}: apply $\Delta$ to the neighbourhood of the touched
    objects, and check required fields and relationships on the result.
    \hfill W2
  \item \textsc{Commit}: for each grounded record that $\Delta$ modifies or
    deletes, require a reason in $\kappa$ and, for a modification, an anchor
    in its paper; for each other interpretive record $o$ that $\Delta$
    modifies or deletes, require $u=\mathit{fb}(o)$. \hfill W4, W5
  \item \textsc{CheckIn}: check the type's light rules on added and modified
    rows; add violations that would corrupt the store to $P$, and the rest to
    warnings $W$.
  \item If $P\ne\varnothing$, return $P$ without writing.
  \item If not \textit{apply}, return $(\Delta, O, W)$ without writing.
  \item If $O\ne\varnothing$ and $\kappa$ gives no reason, return an error.
    \hfill W5
  \item If $\Delta$ is empty, return \texttt{noop}.
  \item In one transaction, write $\Delta$ and the commit
    $c=(u,\mathit{op},m,\Delta,\kappa)$; verify the written state against
    $\Delta$, and abort on mismatch.
  \item Return $c$. \textsc{Commit} also returns the Leaderboards keyed by
    the evaluation datasets of the Experiments that $c$ touched, and the
    datasets that have none.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\label{tab:blocking}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.14\columnwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.40\columnwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.38\columnwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Class & Raised when & Writer's response \\
\midrule
Error & malformed document; unresolvable reference; violated model or type

\subsection{Staleness}
\label{sec:staleness-draft}

Admissibility holds when a write is admitted, but later admissible writes can
change what an Artifact relied on: a paper gains a version, a record is
revised or deleted. PaperWeave neither blocks such writes nor recomputes
Artifacts, whose derivation is an agent's costly and nondeterministic work.
Instead it computes at read time why an Artifact may be stale. Let $v$ be the
current version of $a$, formed by commit $c_{t_v}$ with document $d$, and let
each reference $x\in\mathrm{refs}(d)$ be pinned to the material version
$\mu(x)$ read when $v$ was written. At state $G_t$, if $d$ is missing or its
content hash differs from the registered one, then
$\mathrm{Stale}_t(a)=\{(a,\textsf{document})\}$. Otherwise,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stale}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Stale}_t(a) ={}
  &\{(x,\textsf{removed}) : x\notin G_t\}\\
  \cup\ &\{(x,\textsf{material}) : \mu(x)\text{ is missing or altered}\}\\
  \cup\ &\{(x,\textsf{superseded}) : \mu(x)\text{ has a successor in }G_t\}\\
  \cup\ &\{(x,\textsf{revised}) : \exists s\in(t_v,t],\
    \mathrm{rev}(c_s,x)\}\\
  \cup\ &\{(x,\textsf{stale input}) : x\in\operatorname{Artifacts}(G_t),\
    \mathrm{Stale}_t(x)\ne\varnothing\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $x$ ranges over $\mathrm{refs}(d)$. The predicate $\mathrm{rev}(c,x)$
holds when $c$ changes the content, labels, or outgoing relationships of a
grounded or interpretive record $x$. It excludes agents' \texttt{SUPPORTS} and
\texttt{OPPOSES} relationships and relationships that others add to an
interpretive record. Changes to Entities and Concepts, such as a new alias, do
not count, so that the hint is not drowned in identity maintenance. The
recursion over Artifacts is memoized, and a revisit ends it. Staleness is a
hint: nothing is modified or deleted. \textsc{Search} and \textsc{Traverse}
attach it to every Artifact they return.

\section{Evaluation Measures (Draft)}
\label{sec:eval-draft}

\paragraph{Multi-writer cost (E1).}
The same paper sequence is written by one pseudo-user or by four, with the
same papers, order, seeds, and assignment. For a measure $Y$ where higher is
better, either probe accuracy or $-\mathrm{Loss}$, the multi-writer cost of an
arm at a checkpoint is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:multiwriter-cost}
\Delta_{\text{arm}} = Y_{\text{arm}}(1) - Y_{\text{arm}}(4).
\end{equation}
E1 holds if $\Delta$ for PaperWeave is significantly smaller than $\Delta$ for
files under Git.
% 注意：evaluation_draft.md §4.2 写作 Y(4) - Y(1)，又要求 Δ_A3 显著小于 Δ_A0；
% Y 越大越好时两者方向相反。这里取 Y(1) - Y(4)，待与评测草案对齐。

\paragraph{Knowledge loss.}
Let $R_t$ be the result rows in the store at checkpoint $t$, and $C^{ok}_t$
the cells among them that agree with the source. The knowledge loss between
consecutive checkpoints is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss}
\mathrm{Loss}(t)=\frac{\lvert C^{ok}_t\setminus C^{ok}_{t+1}\rvert}
  {\lvert C^{ok}_t\rvert}.
\end{equation}
Loss is reported together with coverage: an empty store loses nothing.

\paragraph{Sufficiency (E2).}
For each intent $I$, the same probes are answered by readers with the store
only and with the store and papers:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sufficiency}
\rho_I=\frac{\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{store\_only}}(I)}
  {\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{with\_papers}}(I)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Amortization (E3).}
With $C_{\text{build}}$ the total cost of construction and maintenance, and
$c_{\text{base}}$ and $c_{\text{ours}}$ the per-use cost of a baseline and of
PaperWeave, the break-even number of uses, defined when
$c_{\text{base}}>c_{\text{ours}}$, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breakeven}
N^{*}=\frac{C_{\text{build}}}{c_{\text{base}}-c_{\text{ours}}}.
\end{equation}
